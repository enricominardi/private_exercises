1 Introduction¶

The aim of this project is to assess whether some of the following statements are supported by evidence from Gapminder's datasets or not.

"Transport is Europe's biggest source of CO_2, responsible for the emission of over a quarter of all greenhouse gases. Transport emissions have increased by a quarter since 1990 and are continuing to rise with 2017 oil consumption in the EU increasing at its fastest pace since 2001. Unless transport emissions are brought under control national 2030 climate goals will be missed. To meet the 2050 Paris climate commitments cars and vans must be entirely decarbonised. This requires ending sales of cars with an internal combustion engine by 2035. Such a transformation requires wholesale changes, not only to vehicles but also how they are owned, taxed and driven."

Source: PDF, page 2 1.

From this text, we have selected the following claims:

  • Claim 1 - Transport is Europe's biggest source of $CO_2$;
  • Claim 2 - Transport emissions have increased by a quarter since 1990;
  • Claim 3 - Transport is responsible for the emission of over a quarter of all greenhouse gases.

1.1 Structure of the Argumentation¶

Claim 1 - Transport is Europe's biggest source of $CO_2$¶

Basically, the object of interest is the ratio of $CO_2$ emission from the transport sector to the total $CO_2$ emission in Europe, per year.

On the Gapminder website, we can find the following datasets:

Nr. Dataset Name File Name
1 CO2 emissions from transport (% of total fuel combustion) en_co2_tran_zs.csv
2 CO2 emissions from electricity and heat production, total (% of total fuel combustion) en_co2_etot_zs.csv
3 CO2 emissions from manufacturing industries and construction (% of total fuel combustion) en_co2_manf_zs.csv
4 CO2 emissions from residential buildings and commercial and public services (% of total fuel combustion) en_co2_bldg_zs.csv
5 CO2 emissions from other sectors, excluding residential buildings and commercial and public services (% of total fuel combustion) en_co2_othx_zs.csv

We will extract the data for EU countries and compare the mean share of $CO_2$ emission per sector.

Claim 2 - Transport emissions have increased by a quarter since 1990¶

Gapminder offers a dataset of yearly $CO_2$ emissions per country (in kt).

Nr. Dataset Name File Name
1 CO2 emissions (1000 tonnes) yearly_co2_emissions_1000_tonnes.csv

We will calculate the sum of yearly $CO_2$ emission in the EU for years from 1990 to the most recent, common year available.

Thereafter, we will apply the shares of $CO_2$ emission from the transportation sector (calculated as mentioned in 'Claim 1') in order to obtain the level value for each reference year and compute the increase from 1990.

Claim 3 - Transport is responsible for the emission of over a quarter of all greenhouse gases¶

A dataset of total greenhouse gas emissions ($CO_2$ equivalent) is also available (same scale, kt):

Nr. Dataset Name File Name
1 Total greenhouse gas emissions (kt of CO2 equivalent) en_atm_ghgt_kt_ce.csv

We will use the results of 'Claim 2' and compare the $CO_2$ emissions from the transportation sector to the $CO_2$-equivalent emissions of all greenouse gasses.

For 'Claim 2' mentions year 1990 as reference, we will limit each dataset to years from 1990 up to the most recent year. We will keep all the years in betweeen, so that we can inspect trends and make visualizations.

Therefore, we keep data from 1990 to 2014 in all datasets but 'ghg', where the last year will be 2012.

Befrore doing so, we have to create the column lists for the new dataframes. We will create a list of years, by leveraging on Numpy's arange function, add the country name in first position and trim a column list for the 'ghg' dataset, so that it ends with '2012'. Finally, we filter and apply the new column names to the original dataframes and overwrite them in our collection.

5 Conclusions¶

By making use of Gapminder's datasets, we have assessed few statements contained in the report "$CO_2$ Emissions from Cars: the facts" of the European Federation for Transport and Environment AISBL.

In particular:

  • Claim 1 - Transport is Europe's biggest source of CO_2;
  • Claim 2 - Transport emissions have increased by a quarter since 1990;
  • Claim 3 - Transport is responsible for the emission of over a quarter of all greenhouse gases.

The datasets used concerned the shares of $CO_2$ emission per industry sector, the total of $CO_2$ and greenhouse gas emissions. We have limited our analysis to EU counries and considered a time range from 1990 to 2014 (2012 for the greenhouse gas emissions).

After data preparation, we inspected each dataset's mean trend and boxplots. Then, we have assessed the report claims.

5.1 Results¶

  • General Results

We have found out that, in 2014, the sector of electricity and heating has had the highest share of $CO_2$ producition from fossil fuels, followed by transport, manufactring services, building and other services.

The transportation sector registered the highest increase in share (from an inital 19%, to over 31% in 2014), wherease manufacturing the highest decrease (from 20% to about 13%). The other secors recorded overall slight decreases from their respective 1990 values.

  • Report Claims

By looking at Gapminder's data on $CO_2$ production share by sector, we can affirm that transportation is not the biggest source of CO2 emission in the EU.

Concerning Claim 2, we have seen that even though the $CO_2$ emissions have overall dropped from 1990 onwards, those originated from the transportation sector have indeed increased. However, the overall increase was equal to 10.57% and not 25%.

When taking the greenhouse-gas $CO_2$-equivalent emissions into account, the claimed value of more than 25% of greenhouse gas emissions coming from the transportation sector does not find evidence from these data, at first. The maximum share value is 21.66%.

5.2 Limitations¶

Generally, the Gapminder's datasets that we have used provide records up to year 2014 (2012 for Greenhouse gas emissions), wherease all report's claims have been made in 2018. Of course, the lack of more-recent data possibly biases our analysis.

Nevertheless and regarding Claim 1, the difference between the share of energy and heating and the share of transportation is very high (about 10% in 2014). It is unlikely that this gap has been closed in 3 (or even in 4) years. Indeed and as an indication, some online sources show that there is still a gap in 2016 (reference 3).

Concernig Claim 3, we have pointed out that the amount of greenhouse gas emissions from the transportation sector are likely underestimated, for other greenhouse gases from fuel combustion are not taken into account, like methane ($CH_4$), nitrous oxide ($N_2O$) or hydrofluorocarbon.

Furthermore, we have to consider that the share of $CO_2$ from the transportation sector may have increased further in more recent years as well.

Therefore, we cannot empirically refute 'claim 3', for Gapminder's data are not sufficient alone to perform an exhaustive anlysis.
